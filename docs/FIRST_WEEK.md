# The first week, and everything after it

Status: test build for outside testers, updated October 12, 2026. The first week is the guided opening; play carries
on past it with seasons, three sports, breeding, the Hollowmere Cup, retirement and the story of Grandpa's torn pages
(sections below, in the order they were added). A short guide written for testers is in
[TESTER_GUIDE.md](TESTER_GUIDE.md).

## What happens

1. **Grandpa's letter**: three illustrated pages (the existing story art).
2. **Arrival**: walk round the kennel. Read the ledger in the office, see the empty runs and the pantry, and paint your
   kennel's name on the gate sign. An orange arrow and the objective card always show the next step.
3. **Larchwood Rescue**: play ball with each of three dogs (genetically distinct in looks and talents), then choose and
   name one.
4. **Home**: fill the bowl (the dog walks over and eats), give a pat, and teach a first lesson at the field gate. Bed
   ends the day.
5. **Day 2**: Mara, Grandpa's old friend, visits. She invites you to Sunday's Village Fun Day and asks you to find her
   lost keys: the scent-search tutorial in her orchard.
6. **Days 2 to 6**: choose how to spend each morning, afternoon and evening. Options: lessons and retrieve practice in
   Grandpa's field, noticeboard jobs for money, the village shop (food, a brush, a long line), and restoring Grandpa's
   scent garden to unlock the Search and indicate lesson.
7. **Day 7: the Village Fun Day**: three rounds (a mark, a search, a blind) against Victor Sterling's trained Labrador
   and young Billy Ashby's beagle cross. Rivals play the same simulation with their own dogs and handling skill.
   Afterwards Mara points out what your dog showed a gift for, and play continues.

## After the first week (added October 3)

- **Seasons and aging:** seven days make a season (Spring, Summer, Autumn, Winter). At each change of season every dog
  ages three months and a season recap shows what changed: activities, money, skills gained, trial results and ages.
  The top bar shows the season and week.
- **Larkspur trials:** from the second Sunday, a field trial runs every Sunday at the Larkspur trial ground (a new van
  destination). Three rounds against three rival kennels who come back week after week. Entry fee and prize money
  for the top three. A qualifying run needs at least 50 in every round and 165 in total; two qualifying runs earn
  the title for that level, and the dog moves up: Novice (FN), then Open (FO), then Excellent (FX).
  Novice: a single mark, a search and a short blind ($10 entry). Open: a double, a windy search and a water blind
  across the lake ($20). Excellent: a double into cover, a big search, and a blind past an old fall ($30).
- **More dogs:** after the Fun Day, Larchwood Rescue has new arrivals each season ($40 adoption fee; up to six dogs,
  one per run). Dogs not out with you wait in their runs with their names painted over the doors. At the runs you
  choose who comes out and feed the others (one meal each). Each dog has its own skills, titles, energy and hunger,
  and each can enter the Sunday trial.
- **Water work:** restore Grandpa's duck pond ($150, from the field gate's Field work tab) to unlock three set-ups:
  Into the pond, Across the pond, and a Water blind. Dogs swim much slower than they run, so a dog with little love of
  water hesitates at the edge or runs round by the bank, which judges count as a serious fault. Catch it with the
  whistle as it veers and cast it back into the water. The dog's Water aptitude now matters.
- **Jobs** come back on the noticeboard each season, and there are more of them.

## Breeding (added October 4)

- **Start:** restore Grandpa's whelping room ($80) at the puppy paddock, east of the kennel runs.
- **Plan a litter:** choose a dam (one of your females, at least 18 months old, resting two seasons between litters)
  and a sire: your own male, or a stud from the stud book (five titled dogs from rival kennels, $60 to $220).
  The forecast shows litter size, the inbreeding coefficient (computed from the pedigree), likely coat colours with
  their odds once both parents are DNA tested ($25 each; studs come tested), and a range for each talent based on
  what you know about the parents, so dogs you have worked a lot give clearer forecasts. Merle to merle pairings are
  refused (double merle health risk).
- **Litters:** puppies arrive five days after mating (2 to 7, smaller for small dams). Each puppy draws its own genes
  from both parents: coat colour by Mendelian inheritance, talents by the infinitesimal model. The dam does lessons
  only while in whelp. Puppies tumble about the paddock with their mother; first impressions of each puppy's
  boldness and keenness show on its card.
- **Puppies' futures:** at three months (the next season) keep a puppy (it needs one of the six runs; field work
  from nine months) or place it with a family for a fee that rises with the parents' titles, the puppy's quality and
  your trial record. Puppies still undecided at six months are placed for half the fee.
- **DNA in plain words (October 13):** a DNA result is shown as what the dog carries without showing ("Carries
  without showing: chocolate and a long coat", or "Carries nothing hidden"), with the breeders' gene codes folded
  away under "Gene codes". The stud book explains that DNA is about looks only and that a carried colour shows in
  puppies only when the other parent has it too.
- **Records:** every dog's sire, dam and grandparents, the breeder, its litters and (once tested) its coat genotype
  appear in the office ledger.
- **Tester tools:** "Skip to the litter" and "Skip until the puppies can leave" appear in the menu when relevant.

## Raising puppies, your stud dogs and the kennel's name (added October 12)

- **Raising sessions:** once a day per litter, from the litter screen at the puppy paddock.
  Choose new sights and sounds (confidence), gentle handling (cooperation), first fetch (drive), follow me (focus),
  or, with the duck pond restored, a paddling pool (water). One puppy at a time shows whether it is keen, unsure or
  worried. Space praises; X makes it easier. Praise the moment a puppy does the brave thing; help a worried one first;
  wait for an unsure puppy whose tail is wagging (doing it alone is worth most); never praise fear. Each session can
  add up to 3 points to that talent per puppy, capped at about +9 over its puppyhood (and −4 if handled badly).
  Coaching names what each puppy's body is saying for the first three sessions.
- **Evaluation day:** once the puppies are three months old, one session where each takes three tests and you say
  what you saw (1 keen, 2 unsure, 3 worried). A correct read gives a clear estimate of that talent; a wrong one only a
  rough note. Use it to choose who to keep.
- **Reputation (0 to 100):** qualifying runs (+4), titles (+10), trial wins (+3) and proud letters (+5) build it. It
  raises puppy placement fees and stud bookings. See it under Kennel in the office ledger.
- **Letters:** each placed puppy's family writes up to twice, from a season after it leaves. What they say depends on
  the puppy's talents and how it was raised.
- **Your dog at stud:** a male with at least one title can be offered in the Stud book tab. Each new season other
  kennels may book him (fee from his titles and your reputation). Three or more seasons later one of his puppies may
  run for that kennel at Larkspur, with genes that really are half his.
- **Kennel type:** once five puppies have been bred, the ledger says what the kennel's dogs are becoming known for.

## Agility (added October 12)

- **Start:** restore Grandpa's agility ring ($110) from the field gate's Agility tab. It stands in the east corner of
  the training field.
- **How it plays:** you run the numbered obstacles with your dog. The dog takes the obstacle you are running toward,
  so the line you run decides the round. "Go!" / "Go on" (Space) starts the run and sends the dog ahead to the
  obstacle in front of it; "Here" (R) calls it off a wrong one; "Easy" (F) steadies it for a tight turn or the
  A-frame's yellow contact. A course map shows the numbers, you and your dog.
- **Scoring:** 100, less 5 for each bar down, refusal, missed contact or popped weave, 20 for a wrong obstacle, and 2
  for each second over the standard time.
- **Practice:** three courses (four jumps; jump, tunnel and turn; a full ten-obstacle course). A session is three
  runs and costs 20 energy. Obstacles taken cleanly build the Obstacles skill; simple courses stop teaching
  once outgrown (35% and 60%).
- **Shows:** every Saturday from day 13 on the village green: two rounds, the second over the mirrored course.
  A qualifying run is 50+ in each round and 110+ in total; two earn the title (AN, AO, AX). Entry $8, $15, $25.

## Sheep work (added October 12)

- **Where:** Fell Top Farm, by van, any day after the Fun Day. No restoration needed.
- **How it plays:** you stand at the post and work the dog by command: "Come by" (A) sends it round the sheep to the
  left, "Away" (D) to the right, "Walk up" (W) straight onto them, "Lie down" (Space or S) stops it, "That'll do" (R)
  calls it back. Sheep move away from the dog and bunch as they go, so the dog belongs on the far side of the sheep
  from where you want them. Rushing in close scatters the flock.
- **A run:** send the dog round behind the sheep (the outrun), bring them through each pair of gates in order, and
  pen them. The tip at the bottom of the screen says what a good handler would ask for, and that button glows.
- **Scoring (of 100):** outrun 10, gates 40, pen 30, straight lines 10, pace 10, less 4 each time the sheep scatter.
- **Courses:** Gather and pen (3 sheep); Gather, drive and pen (4); the full course with a cross-drive (5, flightier
  sheep, narrower gates). Every run builds the Herding skill: a green dog ignores stops, takes the wrong side and
  creeps; a trained one stops first time and keeps wide.
- **Trials:** every Saturday at Fell Top: two runs, the second mirrored; titles HN, HO, HX. A dog can enter one event
  a day.

## The Hollowmere Cup (added October 12)

- **When:** the last Sunday of winter each year (day 28, 56, …), at Larkspur, in place of the ordinary trial.
- **Entry:** by invitation: reputation 25 or an Open title in any sport, two dogs fit to run, and $40.
- **Format:** four rounds, one of each kind of work: a double mark, a water blind, an agility round, and sheep. Before
  each round you choose which of your dogs runs it; no dog may run more than two. Four rival kennels, each with its
  own strengths, run the same rounds.
- **Prizes:** $400, $220, $120; a win adds 15 reputation and is recorded in the ledger and on the trophy shelf.

## Old age and retirement (added October 12)

- Dogs slow down from nine years (speed and stamina) and more from eleven. Nobody dies.
- From seven a dog can be retired from its page in the office ledger; at twelve it retires itself at the change of
  season. A retired dog lives at the farmhouse, frees its run, and cannot work, compete or breed.
- **Mentoring:** with retired dogs about, working dogs learn faster in lessons, agility and herding: 10% for each
  titled veteran, 4% for others, up to 25%.

## Grandpa's torn pages (added October 12)

- Five pages are missing from the ledger. Each turns up after something you do: restoring the whelping room; a first
  title in any sport; two of the other three restorations; reputation 25; breeding a first litter. They are read
  aloud at the next quiet moment at home and kept under "Torn pages" in the office.
- With all five, and after working Sam Pennock's sheep at least once, "Show Sam the torn pages" appears at Fell Top.
  It brings home Wren, a young black bitch with one white toe: the last of Grandpa's Tam and Nell line, with her
  pedigree recorded. She needs a free run.

## The village and Sandy Cove (added October 12)

- **The vet:** a check-up ($15) measures speed, agility, stamina and power exactly; DNA tests ($25); a scan ($20)
  counts the puppies a dam is carrying. Visits use no time.
- **Breeders' market:** on the first two days of each season, three youngsters (six or nine months old) by stud-book
  sires, $120 to $480, with pedigrees; and kibble by the sack (20 meals, $50). Bought dogs are marked as purchased.
- **Sandy Cove:** the beach. A run on the sand (free play, no time used), and two water marks in the sea. The first
  visit each day adds a little bond.

## Seasons, visitors, photos and trophies (added October 12)

- **Seasons look different:** spring is fresh with blossom on the apple trees, autumn turns the leaves and grass
  gold, winter frosts everything. Each also nudges the work: summer heat tires dogs sooner and carries less scent,
  autumn's damp air holds scent, winter dogs keep going longer. The season recap says so.
- **Visitors at the gate:** Mara every third day with a tip (and two meals if the pantry is nearly empty); Sam on a
  Friday with an invitation to Fell Top; Dora about the agility ring; Victor Sterling the day you have a titled dog.
  A toast says who is at the gate; walk up and press E.
- **Dogs in the runs** get up, turn to watch you and wag as you come near, and settle when you leave.
- **Photos:** the Photo button (top right) saves the view to an album of twelve at the farmhouse.
- **Trophy shelf:** at the farmhouse, everything the kennel has won or restored.
- **The handbook:** Menu → How to play gathers every how-to card.

## Systems in the slice

- **Time and energy (changed October 12):** energy is the only limit on a day's work, and every dog has its own.
  A dog works until it is worn out: about six lessons, or four pieces of field work, from full (lesson 15, mark or
  blind 24, search 22, agility session 20, sheep run 22). When one dog is done, take another out at the kennel runs.
  The clock (morning, afternoon, evening) still moves on with the first two things you do, but it never stops you;
  the earlier rule of three activities a day for the whole kennel is gone. A dog can enter one competition a day.
  "Rest together a while" at the farmhouse gives the dog with you 15 energy back, once a day. Bed starts the next
  day; every seventh day the season turns.
- **Care:** fullness falls as the clock moves and a little with each piece of work; filling the bowl uses a meal from
  the pantry. A night's sleep restores 70 energy (40 for a hungry dog), so a dog worked to nothing every day starts
  the next one short. Hungry dogs listen less and give up sooner; tired dogs run slower and tire sooner.
- **Money:** start with $40 and 4 meals. Jobs pay $15 to $34. A bag of kibble (8 meals) costs $24. The scent garden
  costs $90 and the duck pond $150. Trial entry is $10 to $30; prizes $15 to $160. Later adoptions cost $40.
- **Getting to know your dog:** aptitudes start unknown. Watching your dog work narrows each one to a range shown in
  the office ledger, with "You're getting a feel for..." moments when one becomes clear.
- **Activities:** retrieves (marks, doubles, blinds, with whistle, casts, wind and scent), scent search (choose where to
  search, read the dog, trust or doubt its indications) and five marker-training lessons.

## Tester tools

Menu → Tester tools: +$200 and food; skip a day, to Saturday (shows), to Sunday (trial) or to Cup day; skip the first
week (gives a dog and opens everything after the Fun Day); train every dog to 75%; age every dog a year; skip to the

aptitudes.

## Lessons and trial guidance (added October 7)

- **Coaching in lessons:** while a dog's skill in a lesson is below 40%, the moment its response finishes a ring
  flashes round it and the prompt says what to do: green "Now! Yes!" for the real thing or a step worth rewarding at
  this stage, red "Don't reward that one" otherwise. It fades out as the skill grows, so the timing becomes yours.
- **Beginner dogs give you something to reward:** an untrained dog now offers a hesitant sit, a creep, a glance back
  at the whistle, a near-miss at the scent boxes, or the right pile about half the time, and improves from there.
  Marks within a tenth of a second early count as on time.
- **The goal card after the Fun Day** says exactly what earns a title: two qualifying runs, where a qualifying run is
  50+ in every round and 165+ in total. It lists the dog's Steady, Stop whistle and Directions levels against a
  target for the level (Novice: 50%, 35%, 25%; proposals), when and where the next trial is, and the entry fee.
- **How trials work** card after the Fun Day (and once when loading an older save), also linked from the van.
- **After each trial** the standings name the weakest round and what to practise for it.

## Finding your way at home

- Every place you can use has a ring on the ground and its name floating above it. When you are close enough, the
  ring turns orange and the label says "press E" (on a phone, the big button).
- The farmhouse has one door: Grandpa's office, going to bed and resting together are all inside. The puppy paddock
  is entered from its south side, facing the yard.

## Seeing what matters in the field

- When you stand still, your keeper turns to face what matters: the item in the air, then your dog when it is out, then the newest fall or the blind stake.
- The camera rises and pulls back far enough to fit you and the farthest important thing in front of you.
- Falls (white), blind stakes (orange), the search area (gold) and your dog (when more than 12 m away) carry a light beam and a label with the distance. Anything off screen gets an arrow at the edge of the screen.
- Changing place fades briefly ("Driving to…") so the button responds at once while the next place is built.

## Known limitations

- Buildings, trees, hedges, fences and yard items are Blender-made (`public/models/props.glb`, laid out in `src/render/world/PropWorld.tsx`). Foliage is smooth blobs that don't sway yet, windows and doors are solid, and props have no collision except the buildings at home.
- Characters are Blender-made but still procedural art: no blinking or facial expressions yet, and some foot sliding at unusual speeds. A hired artist could replace the base models later without changing the game.
- Not built: accounts, cloud saves, purchases and leagues; the show ring, tracking and the other sports in the
  vision; regional events; keeper skills; a Hall of Fame. Every rule, price and score is a first proposal, open to
  tuning. Whether a puppy inherits a parent's "premium touched" record is set to yes for now; the fair-league rule is
  still open.
- The handler does not move during sheep work, and sheep and agility equipment are simple code-built shapes.
- Photographs are stored inside the save (about 30 KB each, twelve at most); a cloud version should store them apart.
- Saves are local to the browser (IndexedDB database `paws-rebuild`, version 5; older saves upgrade on load).
